## What’s Working Well
- The lime, charcoal, serif accents, small mono labels, and fine rules maintain continuity with the existing portfolio style.
- “Emerging specialization” and “Learning in progress” clearly qualify the Frappe / ERPNext claims. The copy describes transferable experience without implying shipped ERPNext work.
- Email, phone, GitHub, and LinkedIn links are visible and actionable; the contact invitation explicitly welcomes Frappe / ERPNext opportunities.
- Responsive layouts remain within the viewport, and the mobile contact section keeps each link readable and easy to tap.

## Issues Found
### Issue 1: Frappe direction arrives late
- **What**: Frappe / ERPNext appears after the skills, experience, and insurance-work sections.
- **Where**: Hero and Frappe section.
- **Why it matters**: A recruiter looking specifically for the stated career direction may not discover it until deep into the page.
- **Suggested fix**: Add a brief, explicitly qualified Frappe / ERPNext learning-direction cue in the hero or professional snapshot, retaining the current “learning in progress” disclosure.

### Issue 2: Tablet hero headline wraps awkwardly
- **What**: At 768px, “Building software” breaks into two lines, making the hero headline noticeably taller and tighter beside the portrait.
- **Where**: Hero at tablet width.
- **Why it matters**: The desktop headline composition loses some of its deliberate rhythm at this breakpoint.
- **Suggested fix**: Reduce the headline size or stack the hero columns earlier around the tablet breakpoint.

## Priority Fixes for Next Attempt
1. Surface the qualified Frappe / ERPNext direction earlier without implying professional ERPNext experience.
2. Refine the 768px hero layout to preserve the headline’s intended line grouping.

## Should the next attempt REFINE or PIVOT?
REFINE. The current direction is coherent, responsive, and credible; the remaining improvements are about target-role discoverability and tablet composition, not a change in visual concept.
